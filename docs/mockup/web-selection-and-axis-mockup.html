Selection, progress and the axis

Seven design changes for PowerRoadmap, from the walkthrough on 29 October 2026. Each one states the problem, shows the current build as captured, and proposes the replacement. Nothing here is built yet — this page exists so you can reject a direction before it costs anything.

v2.22.0 Before: captured from the running build After: proposal, not shipped

How to read it. Every “before” is a real screenshot of v2.21.0 taken from the dev server — not a drawing of one. Every “after” is a live mockup drawn in this page, so three of them you can actually use: drag the progress edge, switch the delimiters, pick a handle layout.

What I need from you. A yes or a no per section, and an answer to the four questions at the end. Those four are genuine forks where I would otherwise be guessing.

One thing worth knowing up front. Most of what you hit is not a missing design. It is a design this product already adopted for the PowerPoint surface in v2.6.2 and never implemented on the web. Where that is the case the rule is cited, like SR-IXC-01.

01 · ChartSR-IXC-09

Selection you can see

“I click on ECU extract. There is no highlighting. I’m clicking but nothing happens, just the bottom bar is updated.” … “If I press escape, it seems not to do anything.”

Both halves of that are one defect, and it is not a missing design. The chart does not repaint when the selection changes. The two captures below are the same click: the first taken straight after it, the second after a scroll forced a redraw. The chrome was there all along.

So the fix is a repaint, not a restyle. The one real design change you asked for is the second: the band should cross the whole chart, not stop at the rail.

Proposed. Row band at 7% indigo across the full width, rail and plot together — the value chromeRowWashSelect already carries in the token file. A selected bar keeps the 1px indigo frame it has now. Escape and a click on empty space clear both, and the chart repaints the instant either happens.

02 · ChartSR-IXC-02

Resizing without fighting the anchors

“It’s tricky because we should first select, but then when we select, we have the anchor to do the dependency. The anchors are very good, but we are missing that option to resize.”

You found a real collision. Look again at the second capture above: the two round dependency anchors sit exactly on the bar’s end caps — which is the only place a resize grip can go. The end of a selected bar currently belongs to the anchor, so resizing only works when the bar is not selected, which is why it felt backwards.

Three ways out. Try each and tell me which one you want.

My pick: anchors outside. The bar’s own ends stay the resize grips, which is where every Gantt tool puts them and where your hand already goes. The anchors float just clear of the ends on a short lead, so they never share a pixel with a grip.

03 · ChartSR-IXC-01SR-IXC-06

Progress by dragging the edge

“Right now the progress completion, I can only do it with the plus 10, plus 20. This is not actually very, very good. We have to find a new way.”

There is already a way, decided for this product in v2.6.2 and never built on the web. SR-IXC-01 does not merely prefer dragging — it forbids steppers for continuous values, in those words: “stepper buttons, +/− incrementers, or similar discrete controls for continuous values are forbidden.” SR-IXC-06 then specifies the live percentage readout beside the handle.

The −10% and +10% buttons in your command bar have been against the product’s own interaction spec since the day they shipped.

Proposed. The fill edge becomes a grab handle whenever the bar is hovered or selected. Dragging it sets any value in one gesture, with the percentage shown above the handle while you drag. The two stepper buttons come out of the command bar.

04 · Command barno rule — your call

The command bar, rebuilt

“I do not like at all the bottom toolbar. Actually, probably you need to redesign it because it’s bad. The colors, the color pickers are very big.” … “Select for quick actions, right-click for the rest — this is dumb. Remove this.”

Four things go: the selection’s name, the two stepper pairs, the hint, and about half the width of the swatches. One thing arrives: a chip saying which tool is armed, so an insert mode can never be invisible again.

  • The name goes. The chart says what is selected now that it repaints.
  • The steppers go. Dates are dragged (SR-IXC-02), progress is dragged (SR-IXC-06).
  • The hint goes. It explained the bar to someone who was already looking at it.
  • Swatches halve, from 32px to 16px, in two rows of four.
  • An armed-tool chip arrives, on the left, where the name used to be.

05 · Axisyour decision, taken

Delimiters and weekends

“I zoomed to a weekly level. There are no delimiters. I can see the scale, that is good, but I also need to be able to define the delimiters between the scales.” … “Do I want to show the weekends as normal, or as separate?”

The chart draws no vertical gridlines at all — only the today line and the markers you place by hand. Weekends are shaded, at roughly one percent contrast, which is the same as not. This is also why a dragged milestone felt like it was floating: there was nothing to land on.

Delimiters
Weekends
Taken as you decided: a per-roadmap setting, not automatic. The delimiter granularity is yours to pick and is independent of the zoom, so you can ask for month boundaries while reading days. It saves with the document.

06 · Axisno rule — your call

The today marker, off the labels

“There is a today marker, but the today label itself prevents me from actually seeing where we are in the month. It’s overwriting the labels.”

Correct, and at every scale. The pill is painted into the finest calendar band, so it buries whichever month, week or day number it lands on. In the capture it is sitting on November.

Proposed. The calendar bands belong to the calendar. The pill moves just below them, riding the top of the plot area, and the line continues through the bands as a 1px rule. Nothing in the axis is ever covered.

07 · Chartyour proposal

A band at the foot of the chart

“All the labels for the deadline marker should appear at the bottom of the chart, and I should be able to select them, edit them, and move them around.” … “I tried to place the label below, but because there is no row below, I don’t see my label anymore. That’s dumb.”

Two problems with one answer. A deadline marker draws no hit target at all today, so it cannot be clicked, moved or renamed — its menu exists but is unreachable. And a label pinned below a bar is drawn into the next row’s band, so on the last row it falls past the clip and vanishes.

Your proposal solves both: give the chart a foot band that belongs to this kind of label.

Proposed. A band at the foot holds every deadline label and every below-label that would otherwise fall off the sheet. Each is selectable, renameable in place and draggable along its own marker. The band only appears when something needs it.

Also · already decidedremoval, not a design

The thing you couldn’t name

“There’s like a permanent bar at the bottom that’s like underneath the toolbar. I don’t know actually what it is. I can select it, but I don’t know what that is.”

It is the minimap. It ships on, and it is pinned to the foot of the chart at exactly the height of the floating toolbar — and painted behind it. The only part that is ever visible is the overhang past the ends of the pill, which is what you saw.

  • No label, no title, no tooltip. Nothing on it says what it is.
  • A click on the exposed end pans the chart. One click moved the view nine months.
  • That jump is not undoable.
Decided: it goes. You could not identify it, it is almost entirely hidden, and its only reachable behaviour is an unrecoverable view jump. Removing it is one task rather than the five that fixing it would take.

Four questions · I would be guessing otherwise

Where does an insert land when a section row is selected?

Today every insert lands on the document’s first row, which is usually a section header and often off-screen — that is why your tasks appeared at the section level.

Should milestone, bracket and deadline labels rename in place?

Row and section names already do, on double-click. The product’s own rule says a bar is one object and clicking its label selects it, so extending it is consistent — but it is more surface.

With the name gone, where do the quick actions live?

Colour, bracket, details and delete currently sit in the command bar beside the name.

Does the foot band hold below-row labels too, or only deadlines?

A label pinned below the last row has nowhere to go. It could drop into the foot band, or it could simply be kept inside its own row.
